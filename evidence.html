Read the Metrics page

Read the tags.

Every number on this site carries one, and a tag is never upgraded because a result looks right. A check that cannot decide returns NOT EVALUABLE, never a pass.

MEASURED
Read off a machine: a bench, a clock, a queue log, a cost counter, or a simulator against a measured arbiter.
SIMULATED
Read in the simulator against a prediction sealed before the deck existed, with the deck and receipt hash named. Nothing built.
DOCUMENTED
From a published source: a design guide, a part datasheet, a dissertation.
DERIVED
Arithmetic on the above, with the inputs named.
ESTIMATE
A judgement with its basis stated. Conservative where it says so.
NOT EVALUABLE
A check that could not decide. Also NO BASELINE and UNSUPPORTED, where no comparison exists.
FAIL
A sealed prediction that missed. Left as written.

What went wrong, and what now catches it

Every item below is a real defect from building the work on this site. Each one produced output that looked entirely reasonable. Each one is now caught by a specific, mechanical check, and those checks are the actual deliverable of an engagement. They are what your team is taught to write in week four, and they are written down and yours to keep.

A correction applied with the wrong sign.

what happened
The polarity of a calibration was assumed rather than measured, so the error doubled instead of cancelling.
nothing flagged it
The routine completed, reported plausible calibration codes, and every plot rendered normally.
the check
Inject a known error, apply the correction both ways, and take the direction that measurably reduces the residue. The sign comes from a measurement, never from the derivation on the whiteboard.

A calculation performed one step away from the thing it was correcting.

what happened
An index off by one meant the correction was computed at the wrong point.
nothing flagged it
The numbers moved in the right direction, just not far enough to notice.
the check
Test against a case whose answer is known analytically, where "nearly right" is visibly not right.

A residual that hid at the edges.

what happened
A correction shifted everything in one direction while the endpoint stayed fixed, so the entire error reappeared in a single place nobody was looking.
nothing flagged it
The region everyone examines looked perfect.
the check
Report interior and boundary metrics separately, always. An average across both hides exactly this.

A setting that failed to propagate.

what happened
Correct at the one configuration that was tested, silently wrong at every other, because a default happened to match the test case.
nothing flagged it
The test passed.
the check
Run every test at three configurations minimum. One passing configuration proves the default, not the code.

A test that could not fail.

what happened
A term appeared twice in the same expression and cancelled itself, so the switch that was supposed to inject a fault did nothing at all.
nothing flagged it
The test passed, with the fault injected and without.
the check
Before trusting any guard, prove that the condition it tests can actually be reached. A check that cannot fail is worse than no check, because it is believed.

A trim range that railed exactly where it mattered.

what happened
The adjustment range had not been scaled for accumulation, so the most significant element, the one the whole mechanism existed for, hit its limit and stopped correcting.
nothing flagged it
Every other element trimmed cleanly.
the check
Report range exhaustion as a first-class result, never as a warning in a log.

A result that passed every objective check and was unusable.

what happened
Counts correct, nothing missing, structure complete, and the arrangement was wrong in a way none of those checks could see.
nothing flagged it
Everything that could be graded, passed.
the check
Separate what can be graded objectively from what needs an engineer's eye, and report both. A checker that pretends judgement is arithmetic will either reject correct work or wave through bad work.

A checker that was itself wrong.

what happened
A verification script counted arguments the way one language separates them while checking a language that separates them differently, and reported a three-argument call as taking one.
nothing flagged it
It produced a number, and numbers look authoritative.
the check
Validate the checker against hand-worked cases before trusting anything it says. The tool that checks your work is work, and needs checking.

Eight failures. Eight checks.

Notice what is not in that list: a bigger model, a better prompt, or a smarter tool. Every one of these was caught by something small, mechanical and boring. A known-answer probe, a second configuration, a separated metric, a check on the checker.

The automation makes it affordable to run checks at a scale nobody would do by hand; the checks are what make the output trustworthy; and an engineer still decides what the results mean.

The checks are the deliverable. Week four of an engagement is where your team learns to write them against your own flow, and the discipline, the standing rules, the gates, the claim-tagging practice, is written down and stays with you whether or not anything else does.
